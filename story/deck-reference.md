# Submission deck reference

Reviewed: 14 November 2026. Chosen official demo: an English deck of at most seven slides.
This is presentation research and a proposed structure, not a claim that the planned features work.

Follow [NASA rules and rubric](nasa-rules-and-rubric.md) first. Exact source records, credibility and access limits are in [sources.md](sources.md).

1. Current written NASA guidance [N01-N02] and terms [N07](sources.md#n07).

## Why the deck fits this project

**Our recommendation:** keep the deck. Lunar Navigator needs readable site/date comparisons, screenshots, a clear source-to-result explanation and a short statement of scope. A deck lets the reader inspect these. This is a fit judgment, not a measured winning advantage.

Both formats have winning precedents: Starflock's official 2022 page links Google Slides in its Demo field [W05](sources.md#w05); SHINE's 2021 page links a video [W06](sources.md#w06). These linked formats were verified from the project pages; the linked recordings/decks were not fully viewed. A winners-only sample cannot estimate whether one format increases the chance of winning.

## What the official deck must communicate

**NASA-required / recommended:** seven slides total including title/intro; English; public access; team/challenge identification; readable visuals showing project, user experience and the enabling agency data. Resolve permitted under-18 credits first. [N02 pp. 6, 10-11]

**NASA-confirmed:** most evaluation is based on the full submission. The deck must communicate the core evidence while the detailed page explains methods, validation and references. [N02 p. 6; N01 p. 7]

## Proposed seven-slide structure

**Our recommendation, not a NASA template.** Revise around the working product. Use verified outputs only.

| Slide | Reader's question | Evidence / visual | Main rubric links |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Project and decision | What is it, who made it, and what challenge does it address? | Project name, concise benefit, team/challenge and permitted member credits; real overview image if ready | Presentation, Relevance |
| 2. Why timing matters | What difficulty does the user face? | A simple site/date scenario explaining why lighting and Earth visibility matter; credit science imagery | Impact, Relevance |
| 3. One usable workflow | What can a user actually do? | Annotated screenshots of selecting site/date and interpreting results; demonstrate audience modes only if implemented | Validity, Presentation |
| 4. One real comparison | What useful difference does the tool reveal? | Two supported sites over the same window; readable values/units; a verified date change and its consequence | Relevance, Impact, Validity |
| 5. How NASA data enables this | Where do the results come from? | Exact source/product -> team processing -> displayed output, with a real example | Relevance, Validity |
| 6. Why this approach is useful and credible | What is distinctive, and what supports trust? | Specific improvement over the comparison workflow; validation evidence, assumptions and scope in plain language | Creativity, Validity |
| 7. Demonstrated value and next steps | What did the team achieve and what comes next? | Measured/observed outcome where available; honest future work; final-project link and concise source credits | Impact, Presentation |

Do not force a separate full team biography, technology-logo slide or thanks-only slide into the limit. The exact allocation can change; the five criteria still need evidence across the deck.

## The story to prepare

**Our recommendation:** select a site/date, understand Sun/Earth conditions, compare another site, change the date, explain the trade-off. A changed condition is enough; do not manufacture a dramatic reversal in which the preferred site changes.

A useful screenshot caption identifies: site(s), date/window and time convention, displayed metric and the conclusion supported by it. Keep units and legends readable. Ask the data owner what each indicator actually measures.

For audience modes, show a real difference in task or explanation: an educator can understand why conditions change; a planner can inspect the detail supporting a comparison. If the modes are planned, label them as planned rather than showcasing them as operational.

## Original examples: observations and limits

### SkySense / Twisters, 2025 [W01](sources.md#w01)

NASA confirms Best Use of Technology [N09](sources.md#n09). The supplied screenshots show a recognizable weather-planning situation, coordinated branding and an actual dashboard image. **Observation:** consequences are easier to understand than a list of weather variables. **Our application:** introduce the decision our comparison helps with.

Avoid copying small screenshot labels or generic NASA/API logos in place of provenance. The conversational success story is a team claim, not independent evidence of forecast performance. The uploaded Twisters PDF has six pages but yielded no extracted text; review here relies on Nick's supplied screenshots, not a complete visual inspection of that PDF.

### Gaia+LEO, 2025 [W02](sources.md#w02)

NASA confirms Global Connection [N09](sources.md#n09). Text from all seven uploaded pages was read. Pages 2, 4-6 explain process, data, optimization and constraints; page 7 reports a scenario. **Our application:** pair an understandable result with its method and assumptions. Its environmental/cost projections are not independently validated here; do not borrow the numbers, universalize them or treat slide text as proof.

### AEDES, 2019 [W03](sources.md#w03)

Its original award-bearing page names users, datasets, processing, initial locations and model results. **Our application:** let the detailed project description carry technical evidence and a clearly bounded initial scope. Its scientific formulas and performance claims were not independently audited.

### Radaway, 2017 [W04](sources.md#w04)

Moses Ong's account describes user interviews and iterative usability work. **Our application:** test whether a non-specialist understands the displayed information. The team's reflection about what differentiated it is first-hand interpretation, not a judge's explanation.

## Professional guidance we retain

**Professional advice:** Devpost's five named judges emphasize eligibility, a balanced response to criteria, user experience and consistency between the demo and written submission [P01](sources.md#p01). They are not identified here as NASA judges. Brandon Kessler recommends brief context, showing the central result and a short statement of potential [P02](sources.md#p02).

**Our application:** show the comparison early, make the key result understandable and leave secondary details in the full description. General organizers' longer pitch/video timings do not apply to this submission.

## Avoid these presentation mistakes

| Avoid | Why / better choice | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A beautiful Moon with no demonstrated comparison | Show the useful action and result it supports | Our application of N06 and N01 |
| A feature catalogue with no user situation | Connect the workflow to a concrete need | P02; our application |
| Unreadable full-screen captures | Crop, enlarge and annotate the relevant result | N02 pp. 10-11; our application |
| Changing site AND date without saying which changed | Keep comparisons interpretable; label the scenario | Our recommendation |
| A universal best/safest-site score without defined criteria | State the objective, measures, weights and limitations, if a score exists | Our validity check |
| Calling geometric sunlight electrical power | Confirm what the output measures before naming it | Our scientific interpretation check |
| Calling Earth visibility guaranteed communications | Confirm the modeled scope and explain omitted link constraints | Our scientific interpretation check |
| Claiming terrain shadows because the Moon looks textured | Ask whether local terrain is included in the calculation | T01; team confirmation needed |
| A mockup passed off as a working screen | Label mockup, prototype and implemented evidence accurately | Our application of Validity |
| Invented user counts, saved costs or comparison speed | Measure them or describe intended benefit without numbers | Our evidence standard |
| Saying first-ever without a credible comparison | Explain the specific demonstrated improvement instead | N01 Creativity; our application |
| An eighth credits/appendix slide | Keep the official demo within seven; detailed sources go on the project page | N02 p. 11 |
| Assuming adult winners' face/name slides fit our team | Apply current under-18 restrictions | N02 p. 12; N07 |

## Deck release checklist

- [ ] Exactly seven or fewer exported pages, including all credits/title/closing material.
- [ ] Correct project/team/challenge identifiers and permitted member credits.
- [ ] Clear problem, user, workflow, comparison, data role, distinctive benefit and scope.
- [ ] Screenshots match the final working version and contain readable values/units/dates.
- [ ] All major claims trace to evidence; no placeholders or unsupported percentages.
- [ ] Data owner confirms interpretations and comparisons.
- [ ] Every image/asset has appropriate rights/credit; SI media visibly identified.
- [ ] Export opens correctly with no cropped labels; story stands alone without narration.
- [ ] A non-team reader can explain the problem and what the comparison reveals.
- [ ] Public deck link tested signed out; same project facts appear on submission page.
