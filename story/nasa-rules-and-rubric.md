## Read this first

The governing sources are the current NASA [Judging & Awards Guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/) [N01](sources.md#n01) and [Project Submission Guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/) [N02](sources.md#n02), both dated 13 November 2026. Their text was read from the PDFs uploaded by Nick. Printed PDF page numbers are used below. The supplied [video transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CV7AYfMRyMM) [N03](sources.md#n03) supplements them; it is not a visual review of the recording.

- **Required:** an explicit eligibility or submission instruction.
- **Recommended:** NASA advice, not a separate eligibility condition.
- **Our application:** a team interpretation; see the other guides for this.
- **Pending:** a fact or ambiguity still requiring confirmation.

Current written NASA requirements and terms take priority over the video, historical guides, winner examples and general advice. A source being official does not make every sentence a requirement. Technical documentation is authoritative about its own system, not competition scoring.

## Judging rubric: the primary evaluation reference

At every judging level, each of the five criteria is evaluated on a 1-5 scale. The current guide does not provide percentage weights or a formula for combining them. Do not import the older 2025 holistic wording as the current scoring procedure. [N01, pp. 5-7; N03, 10:44-11:07]

The following are faithful summaries, not additional rules or verbatim rubric text.

| Criterion | What judges assess | What an outstanding score describes | Location |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Impact | The importance of the problem; quality and extent of the benefit; ability to help or inspire people | An exceptional response to a significant problem, with extensive reach or benefit, and unusually high quality and effectiveness | N01 p. 5 |
| Creativity | Originality, inventive execution, a novel concept or a meaningful improvement to existing ideas | A distinctive, groundbreaking approach that challenges established methods and shows exceptional innovation | N01 p. 5 |
| Validity | Scientific foundation; technical, functional or logical feasibility; ability to achieve the purpose; completeness, practicality and usability | A scientifically sound, feasible, developed solution that achieves its intended purpose and is accessible and ready for practical use | N01 p. 6 |
| Relevance | Direct challenge alignment and meaningful, central use of NASA data | A solution deeply connected to the challenge, whose insights and effectiveness depend on well-used NASA data | N01 p. 6 |
| Presentation | Clear communication of the challenge, solution and importance; engaging structure, storytelling and delivery | A polished, compelling account that makes the purpose and potential impact easy to grasp | N01 p. 7 |

**Presentation includes the demo AND full project.** NASA asks teams to give presentation attention alongside the other criteria. Separately, the submission guide explains that most evaluation is based on the project submission, while the demo contributes to overall evaluation and can strengthen Presentation. These statements support a strong deck AND a detailed project page; they do not establish a numeric weighting. [N01 p. 7; N02 p. 6]

The Validity section acknowledges that solutions may still need refinement. The outstanding-score description is an ambitious standard, not permission to call unfinished or unvalidated work ready for mission use. [N01 p. 6]

## Mandatory submission and eligibility checks

- [ ] Address an official challenge and complete all required project-page fields. [N01 p. 3]
- [ ] Every member is registered, accepted rather than waitlisted, and appears on the team's Members tab. All members, including the owner, share the same Local Event. [N01 p. 3]
- [ ] Athens/local team: 1-6 members. Universal Event: 2-6 members. Membership cannot be added after submission closes. The written guide is more precise here than the video's general minimum of one. [N01 p. 3; N03 7:01-7:17]
- [ ] Submit before 15 November 2026, 11:59 p.m., in the Local Event's time zone. For a Universal team, use the Team Owner's residence time zone. [N02 p. 2]
- [ ] Click **Submit for Judging** and observe the confirmation banner; saving a draft alone is insufficient. Resubmit published changes before the deadline. [N02 pp. 3, 12; N03 1:34-2:02]
- [ ] Provide a public demo link AND a public final-project link without access requests, passwords or registration. [N02 pp. 4, 6-7]
- [ ] Demo is at most seven slides TOTAL, including title/intro/closing material, OR a video of 30 seconds or less. No extra appendix beyond seven in the submitted demo. [N02 pp. 6, 10-11; N04 clarification on closing slides]
- [ ] Use English in the project page and linked materials. A non-English spoken demo video needs English subtitles; slides must be English. [N02 pp. 10, 12]
- [ ] Use at least one NASA data source or resource and explain it specifically. Current GO Team clarification says at least one NASA data source from the selected challenge's Resources section; retain this more specific instruction when choosing evidence. Other partner resources are encouraged, not all compulsory. [N02 pp. 8-9; N05]
- [ ] Cite all outside materials actually used, including code, text, images, tools and open/free resources; have the necessary usage rights. [N02 pp. 9, 12]
- [ ] Agree to the terms and the declaration of original work and complete external-resource credits. [N01 p. 3; N02 p. 12]
- [ ] Exclude images, video, audio and other likenesses of under-18s from the final project and demo. [N02 p. 12]

### Under-18 names: unresolved distinction

The slide instructions request member names [N02 p. 11]. The current legal page additionally restricts recognizable names, voices and likenesses of under-18s [N07, Submissions]. The guide and video explicitly discuss visual/audio restrictions but do not settle the written-name question. Nick is under 18. Obtain an organizer-approved way to credit under-18 members before publishing identifying credits. Do not assume initials, parental permission or an adult winner's example settles this.

### AI / SI use

NASA calls these tools Super Intelligence (SI) in the 2026 guide. [N02 p. 8]

- Assistance through machine learning, chatbots, image generation and language models is permitted.
- The guide distinguishes assistance from having SI originate the team's solution idea. Record the team's original concept and the actual assistance accurately.
- Clearly identify where and how tools were used.
- Generated images/videos need a visible generation watermark. Generated audio, code and data need acknowledgement in descriptive text and metadata.
- Generated content must not contain, use or modify NASA logos, flags, mission identifiers or other NASA branding/design elements.
- The SI form response is for data collection and is not shown to judges. Visible labels, descriptions and metadata within the project ARE visible; originality, intent and execution remain assessable.
- The hidden SI field is not a substitute for required indicators in the project itself.
- Our existing assistance includes research, synthesis and Markdown drafting. The whole team must add its actual tool use; do not select no SI merely because Nick did not write application code.

## NASA's demo recommendations

NASA recommends choosing the story early, assigning a demo lead, using readable visuals and communicating to a viewer with no subject background. The deck should show the project, the user experience and how NASA/space-agency data or technology enables it. Include team name, permitted member credits and selected challenge. [N02 pp. 10-11]

The suggested 30-second video flow covers introduction, challenge/inspiration, project title/tagline, operation/user experience/data and future benefit. It is a storytelling example, not a requirement to narrate slides at those timestamps. [N02 pp. 10-11]

The transcript suggests 50-200 words for the high-level summary; this is not a stated hard word limit. [N03 2:23-2:43]

## Our challenge: keep the purpose central

The official challenge asks for intuitive comparison of lunar landing sites and dates, displaying Sun and Earth against the horizon to explore power potential and direct-to-Earth communication windows. Mission planners, educators and the public are named audiences. [N06](sources.md#n06)

The detailed statement suggests curated/custom sites, date flexibility, comparison, timelines and varied visualizations. Do not turn every suggested feature into a separate mandatory deliverable. Primary challenge summary was accessible through NASA search; detailed text was read from the Athlone organizer reproduction [L01](sources.md#l01), which is lower authority. Existing tools already provide relevant information; our presentation must explain the improvement our workflow actually demonstrates.

## Submission mechanics and optional precautions

Images can be uploaded to the project page as JPG, PNG or GIF, up to 10 MB. Videos and documents must be externally hosted and linked. Test links signed out or in a private browser. [N02 p. 4]

NASA recommends, but does not require, requesting FortiGuard Web Filter Classification for a new project website. Choose a category fitting the final site, enter N/A for company name and submit the classification request. Categorization is FortiGuard's decision, not NASA's. The PDF's printed text does not preserve the form target; retrieve the actual link from the live guide before acting. [N02 pp. 4-5]

The edit lock is a coordination aid, not protection against another teammate overriding it. Save frequently and coordinate who enters the final text. [N02 pp. 3-4]

## Judging progression

Local/Universal selection produces Global Nominees, then Global Judging produces finalists and honorable mentions, then executive judges select global award winners. A local award does not itself guarantee advancement. [N01 pp. 8-11]

| Announcement | Date |
| --- | --- |
| Global Nominees | 3 December 2026 |
| Finalists and Honorable Mentions | 17 December 2026 |
| Global Winners | 26 January 2027 |

The ten awards are Best Use of Science, Best Use of Data, Best Use of Technology, Galactic Impact, Best Mission Concept, Most Inspirational, Best Storytelling, Global Connection, Art & Technology and Local Impact. Executive judges nominate projects for awards; there is no verified strategy of choosing an award with supposedly easier odds. [N01 p. 11]

## Avoid these rule mistakes

| Avoid | Reason / better action | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eight slides because the last one is only references | The demo limit includes the whole deck; use concise credits and the full project references | N02 pp. 6, 11 |
| Submitting the older 60-second rehearsal video | It exceeds the current video limit; our chosen official format is a deck | N02 pp. 10-11 |
| Treating Save Progress as submission | Confirm Submit for Judging and the banner | N02 pp. 3, 12 |
| Private links or a localhost final-project link | Judges need a publicly accessible hosted result | N02 pp. 4, 7 |
| NASA logo or background image as the whole data explanation | Identify meaningful NASA resource use and show its role | N01 p. 6; N02 p. 8; N05 |
| No citations because an asset is free | Free/open material still requires credit and appropriate rights | N02 pp. 9, 12 |
| Relying on old dates, team rules or generic pitch durations | Current written NASA guidance takes priority | N01-N03 |
| Unlabelled generated media or generated NASA branding | Follow the SI requirements | N02 p. 8 |
| An under-18 presenter appearing or speaking in the submission | Use interface visuals and an eligible presentation approach; resolve written credits | N02 p. 12; N07 |

Before-event work: the GO Team allows topic research, resource review and team formation before kickoff, but says project-building must wait until the official event. We have not audited the team's chronology; do not make unsupported compliance declarations. [N05](sources.md#n05)
