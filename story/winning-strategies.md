# General judging and presentation strategies

Reviewed: 14 November 2026.
Purpose: practical lessons for making our actual work clear and persuasive. These improve preparation; none guarantees a prize.

## Authority comes first

The [NASA rules and rubric](nasa-rules-and-rubric.md) override every strategy here. Source IDs resolve to exact links, publishers, credibility and inspection limits in [sources.md](sources.md).

- **Current NASA:** rules and event-specific recommendations.
- **Historical NASA:** corroborating advice, not current instructions.
- **Original submissions / first-hand accounts:** examples and experience, not causal proof of winning.
- **Professional advice:** named judges or established organizers, not NASA rules.
- **Our synthesis:** an application to this team, requiring evidence.

Do not describe general hackathon judges as NASA judges, a local winner as a global winner, or a polished winner's submission as an independent scientific validation.

## Impact without exaggeration

**Our recommendation:** use an explicit chain: named user -> task difficulty -> demonstrated action -> useful result -> intended benefit. A specialist audience can have meaningful benefit; do not invent millions of users to make the project sound important. Separate measured outcomes from hoped-for adoption.

Potential evidence we could gather, if time permits:

- Whether a first-time user correctly interprets a comparison.
- Whether the user can complete the chosen task without help.
- Time to perform a clearly defined comparison, with an honest baseline if claiming improvement.
- Feedback explaining which label, view or result was helpful or confusing.

These are proposed checks, not completed research or statistically representative validation.

## Creativity without novelty theatre

**Our recommendation:** explain what makes this particular approach useful: fast scenario comparison, clear explanations, or a coherent transition between simple and detailed views, if demonstrated. A role selector or 3D globe by itself is not evidence of a meaningful innovation.

Do not add an AI assistant, feature or dramatic effect merely because another winner used it. Any addition should serve the challenge and have evidence behind its benefit.

## Scientific communication checks

These are our claim-review questions, not additional competition requirements.

1. Does the displayed name match the measured quantity?
2. Are the time convention, units and spatial/temporal resolution clear?
3. Does a terrain-looking visualization correspond to actual terrain-aware calculations?
4. Are estimated event times presented with appropriate precision?
5. Is missing data clearly distinguished from darkness or blocked Earth view?
6. Is a preference conditional on an explicit mission objective rather than declared universally best?
7. Can the team reproduce the comparison and explain at least one validation case?

Horizons' manual defines its observing-site/output conventions and notes the absence of refraction modeling for non-Earth sites; terrain treatment must be confirmed separately [T01](sources.md#t01). This documents a system's behavior, not our implementation. LOLA background explains the instrument's lunar surface measurements, not the product/version our team used [T02](sources.md#t02).

## General experts: retain the principle, reject conflicting formats

- **Devpost judge interviews [P01](sources.md#p01):** keep the problem, actual experience and written evidence coherent. The article covers different organizations and criteria; it does not establish NASA judging procedure.
- **Devpost founder Brandon Kessler [P02](sources.md#p02):** show resolution to the problem and skip routine account-creation steps when they consume demo space.
- **Devpost participant interviews [P03](sources.md#p03):** leave time to prepare and inspect the final result. Their suggested longer videos and one participant's preference against slides are event/personal advice, not NASA instructions.
- **HackUTD [P04](sources.md#p04):** a clear story and deliberate visuals are useful; its 3-minute pitch/2-minute questions format is irrelevant to our uploaded NASA demo.
- **MLH [P05](sources.md#p05):** show the actual project. Its organizer-oriented science-fair process and 2-minute video recommendation do not apply here.
- **Marina Levay [W12](sources.md#w12):** memorable framing and removing unclear explanations are useful local-event mentoring lessons, not global judging requirements.

## Avoid these strategic traps

| Trap | Better action |
| --- | --- |
| Optimizing for a supposed easiest award | Make the project strong against the actual rubric |
| Treating a winners directory as a probability model | Use it to find original materials, not estimate our odds |
| Assuming winner features caused the win | Identify the useful communication practice and label our inference |
| Copying a winner's unsupported impact numbers | Use evidence for our own scope and benefit |
| Spending the whole deck on ambition | Show current work before future plans |
| Making the written submission repeat slogans | Add method, evidence, assumptions and source detail |
| Presenting intended audiences as real users/adopters | Distinguish target audience, feedback participants and actual adoption |
| Claiming tests or endorsements we have not obtained | Report who checked what, when, with which result |
| Ignoring a weak criterion because the interface looks good | Review the evidence for each of the five criteria |
| Depending on live narration to explain an uploaded deck | Make each page understandable on its own |
| Continuing broad research instead of collecting project evidence | Use this reference, then prioritize screenshots, data provenance and results |
| Editing shared submission text simultaneously | Assign an editor and coordinate save/resubmit steps |

## Advice explicitly rejected

The community collection [C01](sources.md#c01) contains a 2-3-minute demo recommendation, a 1,000-plus-word description suggestion, a universal working-prototype assertion and percentage-based CSE win ratings. None is a reliable NASA requirement or validated chance of winning. Keep its directory role only.

Do not replace the repository licence with MIT because a community guide suggests it. Licensing choices belong to the repository owner and actual applicable requirements; this documentation task does not alter them.

Historical winner deadlines, local pitch times and past branding/AI practices are not substitutes for current NASA instructions. Two past formats having winners does not establish which format is statistically superior.
